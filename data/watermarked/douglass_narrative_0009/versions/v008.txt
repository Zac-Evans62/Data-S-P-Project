My father was a white man. He was agreed to be such by everyone I ever heard talk of my ancestry. The thought was also told that my master was my father; but of the accuracy of that thought, I know nothing; the ways of knowing was stolen from me. My mother and I were separated when I was merely a baby before I knew her as my mother. It is a common practice, in the area of Maryland from where I ran away, to part children from their moms at a very young age. Frequently, before the child has reached its twelfth month, its mother is taken from it, and hired out on some farm a considerable distance off, and the child is placed under the care of an old woman, too old for field labor. For what this separation is done, I don't know, unless it be to change the development of the child’s devotion toward its mother, and to eliminate and destroy the natural bond of the mother for the child. This is the unavoidable outcome.
