“Oh, it won’t work—really it won’t,” remarked Holmes smoothly. “There is no possible getting out of it, Mr. Windibank. It is quite too clear, and it was very bad compliment when you said that it was impossible for me to solve so simple a question. That’s correct! Sit down and let us talk it over.”

Our visitor collapsed into a chair, with a frightful face and a layer of moisture on his brow. “It—it’s not actionable,” he stammered.

“I am very much afraid that it is not. But between ourselves, Windibank, it was as cruel and selfish and heartless a trick in a petty way as ever came before me. Now, let me just run over the course of events, and you will contradict me if I go wrong.”

The man sat hunched in his chair, with his head sunk upon his breast, like one who is utterly beaten. Holmes stuck his feet up on the corner of the mantelpiece and, leaning back with his hands in his pockets, began talking, rather to himself, as it seemed, than to us.
