The raiders poured over the fence like monkeys. Squire and Gray fired again and again; three men fell, one forwards into the enclosure, two back upon the outside. But of these, one was evidently more frightened than hurt, for he was on his feet again in a crack and instantly disappeared among the trees.

Two had seen the end, one had fled, four had made good their foothold within our defences, while from the shelter of the timber seven or eight men, each evidently supplied with several muskets, maintained a hot though useless gunfire on the log-house.

The four who had boarded made straight before them for the building, shouting as they ran, and the men among the trees shouted back to encourage them. Several shots were fired, but such was the hurry of the marksmen that not one appears to have taken effect. In a moment, the four pirates had swarmed up the mound and were upon us.

The head of Job Anderson, the boatswain, appeared at the middle loophole.

“At ’em, all hands--all hands!” he roared in a voice of thunder.
