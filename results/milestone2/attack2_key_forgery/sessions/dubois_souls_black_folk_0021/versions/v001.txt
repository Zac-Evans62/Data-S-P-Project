A “submerged tenth” of croppers, with a few paupers; 40% who are metayers and 39% of semi-metayers and wage-laborers. There are left 5% of paper bill-renters and 6% of freeholders,—the “Upper Ten” of the area. The croppers are entirely without capital, even in the limited sense of food or paper bills to keep them from seed-time to harvest. All they furnish is their labor; the area-owner furnishes the area, stock, tools, seed, and house; and at the end of the year the operator gets from a third to a half of the crop. Out of his share, however, comes pay and interest for food and clothing advanced him during the year. Thus we have a operator without capital and without wages, and an employer whose capital is largely his employees’ wages. It is an unsatisfactory arrangement, both for hirer and hired, and is usually in vogue on poor areas with hard-pressed owners.
