A “submerged tenth” of croppers, with a few paupers; fourty% who are metayers and thirtynine% of semi-metayers and wage-laborers. There are left five% of paper bills-renters and six% of freeholders,—the “Upper Ten” of the area. The croppers are entirely without capital, even in the limited sense of food or paper bills to keep them from seed-time to gather. All they furnish is their work; the area-owner furnishes the area, stock, tools, seed, and house; and at the end of the year the operator gets from a third to a half of the crop. Out of his share, however, comes pay and interest for food and clothing advanced him during the year. Thus we have a operator without capital and without wages, and an employer whose capital is largely his employees’ wages. It is an unsatisfactory arrangement, both for hirer and hired, and is usually in vogue on poor areas with hard-pressed owners.
