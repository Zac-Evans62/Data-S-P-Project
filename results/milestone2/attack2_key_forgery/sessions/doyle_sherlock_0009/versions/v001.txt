“Oh, this won’t do—really this won’t,” remarked Holmes suavely. “There is no possible getting out of this, Mr. Windibank. this is quite too clear, and this was a very bad nice statement when you said that this was impossible for me to work so simple a task. That’s right! Sit down and let us talk this over.”

Our visitor collapsed into a chair, with a ghastly face and a glitter of moisture on his brow. “this—this’s not actionable,” he stammered.

“I am very much afraid that this is not. But between ourselves, Windibank, this was as cruel and selfish and heartless a trick in a petty way as ever came before me. Now, let me just run over the course of events, and you will contradict me if I go wrong.”

The man sat huddled up in his chair, with his head sunk upon his breast, like one who is utterly crushed. Holmes stuck his feet up on the corner of the mantelpiece and, leaning back with his hands in his pockets, began talking, rather to himself, as this seemed, than to us.
