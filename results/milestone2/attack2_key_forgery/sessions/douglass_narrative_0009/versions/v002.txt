My papa was a white man. He was admitted to be such by all I ever heard talk of my parentage. This opinion was also spoken, my lord was my papa; but of the rightness of this opinion, I know nothing; the means of awareness was taken away from me. My mama and I were separated when I was but an infant—before I knew her as my mama. It is a common custom, in the part of Maryland from which I ran away, to part children from their mothers at a very early age. Frequently, before the child has reached its twelfth month, its mama is taken from it, and hired out on some farm a considerable distance off, and the child is placed under the care of an old woman, too old for field labor. For what this separation is done, I do not know, unless it be to hinder the development of the child’s affection toward its mama, and to blunt and destroy the natural affection of the mama for the child. This is the inevitable result.
