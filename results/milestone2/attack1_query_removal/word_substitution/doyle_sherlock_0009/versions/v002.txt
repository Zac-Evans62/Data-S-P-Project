“Oh, it won’t work—really it won’t,” remarked Holmes sharply. “There is no possible chance you can get out of it, Mr. Windibank. It is quite too evident, and it was very a very bad compliment when you said that it was impossible for me to answer such a simple question. That’s correct! Sit down and let us talk it over.”

Our visitor collapsed into a chair, with a scared face and a drop of sweat on his forehead. “It—it’s not punishable,” he stammered.

“I am very scared that it is not. But between us, Windibank, it was as brutal and selfish and callous a trick in a petty way as ever came to me. Now, let me just run over the sequence of events, and you may correct me if I am wrong.”

The man sat hunched in his chair, with his head sunk upon his chest, like one who is wholly beaten. Holmes stuck his feet up on the corner of the table and, leaning back with his hands in his pockets, began talking, rather to himself, as it seemed, than to us.
