The team of ten dogs was unhitched, and Buck, with his own harness, was put into the sled. He had caught the fever ofexcitement, and he realized that in some way he must perform a great expolit for John Thornton. Murmurs of admiration at his splendid appearance rose. He was in perfect condition, without one ounce of excess flesh, and the one hundred and fifty pounds that he weighed were so many pounds of grit and virility. His furry coat shone with the sheen of silk. Down the neck and across the shoulders, his mane, at rest as it was, half bristled and seemed to lift with every movement, as though excess of life made each hair alive and active. The great muscles and heavy fore legs were no more than in proportion with the rest of the body, where the muscles showed in tight rolls underneath the skin. Men felt these muscles and said they were hard as metal, and the odds went down to two to one.
