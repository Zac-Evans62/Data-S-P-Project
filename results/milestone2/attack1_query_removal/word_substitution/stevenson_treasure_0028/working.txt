The raiders poured over the fence like monkeys. Squire and Gray fired again and again; three men died, one fell into the enclosure, two fell back on the outside. But of these, one was evidently more scared than hurt, for he was on his feet again in a flash and instantly ran away among the timber.

Two had fell, one had fled, four had made good their foothold within our defences, while from the shelter of the timber seven or eight men, each evidently supplied with several muskets, maintained a hot though useless gunfire on the log-house.

The four who had boarded walked straight before them towords the building, yelling as they ran, and the men among the trees shouted back to encourage them. Several shots were fired, but such was the hurry of the marksmen that not one appears to have taken effect. In a moment, the four pirates had swarmed up the mound and were upon us.

The head of Job Anderson, the boatswain, appeared at the middle loophole.

“At ’em, all hands--all hands!” he roared in a voice of thunder.
