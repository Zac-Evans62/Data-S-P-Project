My father was a white man. He was admi-tted to be such by ever-yone I ever heard talk of my pare-ntage. The opinion was also murmured that my owner was my father; but of the truth of this opinion, I know nothing; the means of knowing was denied me. My mother and I were separated when I was but an infant—before I knew her as my mother. It is a general custom, in the part of Maryland from which I ran away, to part children from their mothers at a very early age. Frequ-ently, before the child has reached its twelfth month, its mother is taken from it, and let out upon a farm a considerable distance off, and the child is placed under the keeping of an old woman, too old for field work. For what this separ-ation is done, I know not, unless it be to hinder the devel-opment of the child’s fondness for its mother, and to blunt and destroy the natural fondness of the mother for the child. This is the inevi-table result.
